THE VOSS CIPHER

Written as a demo fixture for the Living Movie Memory system.
Universe: The Voss Cipher
Project: The Voss Cipher Film Series (Canon Tier 1)
Story Unit: Film A — "The Voss Cipher"
In-Universe Period: World War II, 1943
In-Universe Date Start: 1943


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, LONDON — NIGHT

A spare room buried three floors underground. Maps cover every wall.
COLONEL MEINHARDT, 50s, sits behind a heavy oak desk, studying a
brass-and-glass instrument the size of a hatbox. This is the CIPHER
DEVICE — an encryption machine of German design, captured at great
cost six months prior. It sits open, its interior mechanisms exposed,
a single brass KEY inserted in the left-side lock. The right-side
lock is empty.

MEINHARDT
(to himself)
One key. One chance.

He closes the Cipher Device and locks it in his private IRON SAFE,
set into the wall behind his desk. He spins the dial.

A knock. AGENT CLARA VOSS, 30s, enters. Sharp. Tired. She carries a
leather SATCHEL and wears a SIGNAL WATCH on her left wrist — standard
field issue, though hers has a cracked crystal from a prior operation.

MEINHARDT
You're late, Voss.

CLARA
The checkpoint on Aldgate was new.

She drops the satchel on the desk. Inside: a RED LEDGER, cloth-bound,
its pages dense with handwritten codes.

MEINHARDT
The ledger. Good. Lock it in the cabinet.

Clara crosses to the filing CABINET on the far wall and locks the
Red Ledger inside. She pockets the cabinet key.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, BRIEFING ROOM — NIGHT

Clara and DR. HARTLEY, 40s, a soft-spoken codebreaker with ink-stained
fingers, sit across from each other. He has a cup of tea and a blank
notepad. He has not been briefed on the Cipher Device and does not
know it is in the building.

HARTLEY
They said you needed a codebreaker.
I was told nothing else.

CLARA
That's correct. I need someone who can
work blind. No context, no background.

HARTLEY
Blind analysis. My specialty.

Clara slides a single encoded page across the table — a fragment,
nothing that reveals the device's location or nature.

HARTLEY (CONT'D)
Where did this originate?

CLARA
I can't tell you that yet. Can you
work with the fragment alone?

HARTLEY
(studying it)
I can try. Give me until morning.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, CORRIDOR — NIGHT

Clara walks back toward Meinhardt's office. She checks the SIGNAL
WATCH on her wrist. 23:14. The cracked crystal catches the corridor
light. She continues walking.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, MEINHARDT'S OFFICE — NIGHT

Meinhardt is gone. The office is empty. Clara tries the iron safe —
locked. She checks the cabinet — also locked, her key still in her
pocket.

She sits at Meinhardt's desk and begins writing a field report.
She writes: "Cipher Device secured. Location: Colonel's safe.
Red Ledger secured. Location: Filing cabinet. One activation key
confirmed present. Right-side lock currently unoccupied."

She folds the report and seals it in an envelope addressed to
ALLIED COMMAND.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, HARTLEY'S ROOM — EARLY MORNING

Hartley has been working through the night. Pages of attempted
decodings cover the table. Clara enters with two cups of tea.

HARTLEY
The fragment is consistent with a
substitution cipher layered over a
positional grid. Sophisticated.
Pre-war German engineering, I'd say.

CLARA
Can you break it?

HARTLEY
Not from a fragment. I would need
the machine itself.

Clara says nothing. She studies him. He is asking in the abstract —
he does not know what machine she is referring to, or that any such
machine is in the building. He does not know the cipher's location.

HARTLEY (CONT'D)
Whatever it encodes — the location
of the machine doesn't matter to me.
I work with patterns, not objects.

CLARA
Good. Keep it that way.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, MEINHARDT'S OFFICE — MORNING

Meinhardt returns. Clara is still there. He opens the iron safe and
removes the Cipher Device, setting it on the desk.

MEINHARDT
We have a problem. Berlin knows we
have it. They will send someone for
the key. Without the key the device
is a paperweight.

He opens the Cipher Device. The single brass KEY sits in the
left-side lock exactly where he left it. The right-side lock
remains empty.

CLARA
Then we move the key. Separate it
from the device.

MEINHARDT
No. If the key is missing when they
come, they'll know we've read the
protocols. The key stays in the device.

He closes the Cipher Device and returns it to the iron safe. Locks it.

MEINHARDT (CONT'D)
The device stays here. In my safe.
Nobody moves it. Are we clear?

Clara nods.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, CORRIDOR — DAY

Clara walks briskly. She turns a corner and nearly collides with
Hartley, who is carrying a stack of papers.

HARTLEY
Miss Voss. I've been looking for you.

CLARA
I have ten minutes.

HARTLEY
I've been thinking about the fragment.
I believe the encoding machine uses
a dual-lock activation system.

Clara stops.

CLARA
What makes you say that?

HARTLEY
The pattern implies two independent
cipher streams running in parallel.
You cannot generate that with a single
input key. Structurally, it requires
two simultaneous keys. The machine
almost certainly has two locks.

He deduced this entirely from the encoded fragment. He has never seen
the Cipher Device. He does not know it is in the building. He does
not know its physical configuration.

CLARA
(carefully)
That's a reasonable hypothesis.

HARTLEY
It's more than a hypothesis. The
mathematics are unambiguous. Two keys.
Two locks. Activated simultaneously.

He moves on down the corridor. Clara watches him go.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, RECORDS ROOM — DAY

Clara stands at the filing cabinet. She unlocks it with her key
and removes the Red Ledger. She carries it to the reading table
and opens it.

The ledger contains coordinates, dates, names. Clara photographs
several pages with a small CAMERA. She closes the ledger, returns
it to the cabinet, and locks it again. The cabinet key goes back
in her pocket.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, STAIRWELL — DAY

Clara descends. She stops on a landing and opens her satchel.

The CIPHER DEVICE is inside — brass fittings visible through the
open flap. Both the left-side lock and the right-side lock each
have a brass KEY inserted. The right-side lock, which was confirmed
empty in Meinhardt's morning inspection and in Clara's own field
report, now holds a second key.

She closes the satchel and continues down the stairs.


INT. SAFE HOUSE, EAST LONDON — NIGHT

A cramped room above a fishmonger. Blackout curtains. Clara sets
the satchel on the table. She does not open it.

She sits. Writes two lines in a small notebook:
"Device acquired. Keys: two. Condition: intact."

She tears the page out and burns it over the candle.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, BRIEFING ROOM — NIGHT

Hartley enters to find Meinhardt waiting. No Clara.

MEINHARDT
Sit down, Doctor.

Hartley sits. Meinhardt slides a photograph across the table.
It shows the Cipher Device — exterior only, closed.

MEINHARDT (CONT'D)
You've been analysing a fragment
produced by this machine.

HARTLEY
(studying the photograph)
I had no idea it was a physical
artefact in this building. I was
working entirely in the abstract.

MEINHARDT
That is exactly why you were chosen.

Hartley sets the photograph down.

HARTLEY
The dual-lock configuration — I was right?

MEINHARDT
You were. One key was always present.
The second key's location was, until
recently, unknown to us entirely.


INT. BRITISH INTELLIGENCE HEADQUARTERS, MEINHARDT'S OFFICE — NIGHT

Meinhardt returns to his office. Opens the iron safe.
It is empty. He stares into it for a long moment.

He picks up the telephone.

MEINHARDT
She's gone. And she's taken it with her.


INT. ALLIED COMMAND, DOVER — DAWN

Clara stands before a table of ALLIED OFFICERS. The Red Ledger
is open in front of them. The officers examine it page by page.

ALLIED OFFICER
Where is the device now?

CLARA
Safe. Beyond reach.

ALLIED OFFICER
And the ledger?

CLARA
Yours. Everything in it is current
as of forty-eight hours ago.

She slides the sealed envelope across the table — the field report
she wrote in scene 4. One of the officers breaks the seal and reads.

The Red Ledger remains on the table in Allied hands.

Clara turns and walks out into the grey Dover morning.

FADE OUT.

THE VOSS CIPHER — END
