LEGACY PROTOCOL

Written as a demo fixture for the Living Movie Memory system.
Universe: The Voss Cipher
Project: The Voss Cipher Film Series (Canon Tier 1)
Story Unit: Film B — "Legacy Protocol"
In-Universe Period: Present Day, 2024
In-Universe Date Start: 2024


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, LONDON — DAY

A modern facility behind a Victorian facade. Climate-controlled
vaults. Digital access logs. DIRECTOR SARAH VOSS, 40s, stands
in the reading room studying a glass display case. Inside it:
a brass-and-glass instrument the size of a hatbox, mounted on
a plinth. A placard reads: THE VOSS CIPHER DEVICE. RECOVERED 1943.

Sarah is Clara Voss's granddaughter. Same jaw. Different century.

An archivist, COLE, 30s, stands nearby with a tablet.

COLE
First time seeing it in person?

SARAH
My grandmother never described it
as small. I always imagined something
the size of a piano.

COLE
The legends grow. That's what eighty
years does.

Sarah studies the device through the glass. Both lock slots are
visible — left and right. Both are empty. The keys were separated
from the device decades ago for security reasons.

SARAH
Where are the keys?

COLE
That's actually why your office
called mine.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, DIRECTOR'S OFFICE — DAY

Sarah sits across from ARCHIVE DIRECTOR PEMBERTON, 60s, a careful
man who chooses words like a lawyer.

PEMBERTON
Three weeks ago a private collector
in Zurich put an item to auction.
A single brass activation key,
provenance documented to 1943.
We believe it is the original key
from the Cipher Device.

SARAH
There was only one key.

PEMBERTON
That was the official record, yes.
Your grandmother's field report —
which you've read, I assume —
stated one key confirmed present.

SARAH
I've read it many times.

PEMBERTON
The Zurich key matches the left-side
lock exactly. Our metallurgical
analysis confirms it.

Sarah leans back.

SARAH
So someone has had it for eighty years
and only now decided to sell it.

PEMBERTON
It appears so.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, READING ROOM — DAY

Sarah sits alone at a long table. In front of her: photocopies of
Clara's original field report from 1943. She reads the line she has
read a hundred times: "One activation key confirmed present.
Right-side lock currently unoccupied."

She turns the page. A separate document — a post-war intelligence
assessment, classified until 2019. She reads a highlighted passage:
"The Voss Cipher Device requires two simultaneous activation keys
to operate. Neither key alone is sufficient to engage the mechanism.
Both locks must be turned in concert."

Sarah sets the document down.

SARAH
(to herself)
Two keys. Gran, what did you do
with the second one?


INT. MARCUS WEBB'S OFFICE, WHITEHALL — DAY

MARCUS WEBB, 50s, Deputy Director of a department with no public
name, reads the same post-war assessment across a desk from Sarah.

MARCUS
The two-key requirement was kept
out of the original field reports
deliberately. Meinhardt's decision.
If the Germans recovered the device
and found only one key, they would
assume the second was simply lost.
They would not know the device was
never operable with one key alone.

SARAH
So my grandmother's report —

MARCUS
Was accurate as far as it went.
She documented what she observed.
One key in the left lock. Right
lock empty. She had no way of
knowing the device required both
simultaneously.

SARAH
She took the device. Scene nine —
she had it in her satchel.

MARCUS
We know. We've known for forty years.
What we don't know is where she
acquired the second key, or whether
she ever used the device at all.

Sarah looks out the window.

SARAH
If it requires two simultaneous keys,
and she only ever had one —

MARCUS
Then whatever she encoded, if
anything, is still locked.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, VAULT — NIGHT

Sarah stands before the display case again. After hours. The archive
is empty. Cole has left her his access card.

She studies the right-side lock. Empty for eighty years.

Her phone buzzes. A message from an unknown number.
A photograph: a brass key, right-side profile, with a hallmark
she recognises from the archive's metallurgical report.

A second message: "Reykjavik. Two days."


INT. HOTEL LOBBY, REYKJAVIK — EVENING

Sarah waits in a corner chair. A woman enters — INGRID, 70s,
white-haired, unhurried. She sits across from Sarah without
introducing herself and sets a small wooden BOX on the table.

INGRID
Your grandmother gave this to my
mother in the spring of 1944.
She said: keep it until someone
comes asking about two locks.

Sarah opens the box. Inside: a brass KEY, right-side profile,
identical in style to the Zurich key.

SARAH
She had it all along.

INGRID
My mother always assumed it was
a house key. She kept it in a
drawer for thirty years.

Sarah holds the key up to the light.

INGRID (CONT'D)
There is a condition. My mother
made my grandmother promise.
The key goes to the archive, not
to any government. It goes behind
glass. Not into a lock.

SARAH
I can agree to that.


INT. CHARTERED FLIGHT, SOMEWHERE OVER THE NORTH SEA — NIGHT

Sarah sits alone. The wooden box is in her bag. She opens her
laptop and pulls up a scanned document — a letter in Clara's
handwriting, dated April 1944.

She reads aloud quietly.

SARAH
"The device is intact. Both keys
are accounted for. I have chosen
not to use it. The information it
would unlock died with the men
who encoded it, and some doors
are better left closed."

She closes the laptop. Looks out the window at the black water below.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, READING ROOM — DAY

Sarah and Pemberton sit across from Marcus. The wooden box is on
the table between them. The Zurich key — acquired at auction by
a government front — sits beside it.

MARCUS
So she had both keys from 1943
onward and never activated it.

SARAH
That's what the letter says.

PEMBERTON
Which means the device has never
been operated. Whatever it was
built to encode is still locked
inside the mechanism.

Marcus picks up the Zurich key and turns it over.

MARCUS
The Red Ledger. Your grandmother
handed it to Allied Command in
Dover. It's been in the archive
since 1943. Everything in it was
decrypted by 1947.

SARAH
So the ledger is fully accounted for.

MARCUS
The ledger, yes. The device, now yes.
The question is what the device was
actually built to protect.

He sets the key down.

MARCUS (CONT'D)
We think the Cipher Device was not
an encryption machine. We think it
was a key safe. Built to protect
something small enough to fit inside
the mechanism housing itself.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, CONSERVATION LAB — DAY

A conservator in gloves carefully opens the Cipher Device for the
first time in decades under Sarah's supervision. The mechanism
housing is larger than it needs to be for the cipher function alone.

The conservator lifts a false panel inside the housing.

Inside the hidden compartment: a folded piece of paper, brittle
with age.

Sarah leans in. Reads it. Her expression does not change.

She carefully refolds it and places it in an evidence bag.

SARAH
(to the conservator)
This doesn't go into the catalogue.
Not yet.


INT. MARCUS WEBB'S OFFICE, WHITEHALL — NIGHT

Sarah drops the evidence bag on Marcus's desk.

MARCUS
(reading through the bag)
Good God.

SARAH
My grandmother knew. She had both
keys the entire time and she chose
never to open it. She knew what
was inside and she decided it
should stay locked.

MARCUS
How could she have known without
opening it?

SARAH
She was Clara Voss.

A long silence.

MARCUS
What do you want to do?

SARAH
What she did. Put it back behind
glass. Let it stay closed.

She picks up the evidence bag.

SARAH (CONT'D)
The Red Ledger is in Allied hands
where she left it. The device goes
back in the display case. The keys
go in separately, in the same case,
where people can see them but not
use them. And whatever is in that
document is above both our pay grades.

She walks to the door.

SARAH (CONT'D)
Some doors are better left closed.

She leaves.


INT. NATIONAL CRYPTOGRAPHIC ARCHIVE, DISPLAY ROOM — DAY

A new display case. Three items on three plinths, side by side:

The CIPHER DEVICE.
The LEFT KEY, recovered from Zurich.
The RIGHT KEY, from Ingrid's wooden box.

A new placard: THE VOSS CIPHER. DEVICE AND KEYS REUNITED, 2024.

Cole stands beside Sarah, looking at the display.

COLE
Do you know what's inside?

SARAH
No.

A beat.

COLE
Good answer.

They stand there for a moment. Then Sarah turns and walks out
through the archive's front doors into the London afternoon.

FADE OUT.

LEGACY PROTOCOL — END
